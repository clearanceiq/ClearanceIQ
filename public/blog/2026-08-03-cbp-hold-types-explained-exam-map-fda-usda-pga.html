CBP Hold Types Explained: EXAM, MAP, FDA, USDA, PGA and What Each Means

August 03, 2026 · Estimated reading: 7–9 minutes

Not every customs hold is the same. CBP and partner government agencies (FDA, USDA, CPSC, etc.) apply different hold codes, different release criteria, and different timelines. If your shipment is stuck, the hold code determines whether you need a sample, a certificate, or simply more time.

Before we start: the patterns below are drawn from publicly available CBP instructions and PGA guidance. Hold release timelines vary by port, commodity, and whether the importer of record responds with the requested documentation. Treat this as a planning reference, not a ruling on a specific entry.

EXAM — Intensive physical examination

An EXAM hold means CBP has selected the container or pallet for a full physical inspection. This is the most resource-intensive hold type and the one most likely to generate storage and demurrage costs while the exam takes place.

EXAM holds are initiated under CBP's targeting and examination authority (19 U.S.C. § 1499; 19 CFR Part 151). The shipment is sent to a CBP exam site or a contracted Centralized Examination Station (CES).

Release criteria:

  • No prohibited or undeclared items found during inspection, or
  • Any discrepancy is resolved with the importer (e.g., correct value, correct HTS code).

Common EXAM triggers: high-risk country of origin, incomplete invoice descriptions, unverified supplier history, or random selection under CBP's cargo selectivity model.

MAP — Manifest / Manifest Analysis Program

A MAP hold is applied when CBP's automated systems detect a discrepancy between the manifest data and the entry summary, or when the bill of lading / manifest is incomplete or late.

Under the Focused Select / CES program and Automated Manifest System (AMS), CBP validates that what was declared matches what was loaded. A MAP hold is typically a data problem, not a physical inspection.

Release criteria:

  • Corrected or supplemental manifest data accepted by CBP.
  • Late ISF (Importer Security Filing) penalties may also attach if 10+2 data was deficient per 19 CFR Part 701.

FDA — Food and Drug Administration holds

FDA holds apply to regulated products: food, beverages, dietary supplements, cosmetics, medical devices, human drugs, animal drugs, and radiation-emitting devices. The hold is placed by FDA's Operational and Administrative System for Import Support (OASIS) or the Predictive Risk-based Evaluation and Analysis Import Screening (PREDICT) system.

Authority: FD&C Act, 21 U.S.C. § 381 (import alerts), and FSMA for human/animal food.

Release criteria:

  • FDA Prior Notice submitted and accepted (for food).
  • No active import alert (e.g., Import Alert 99-19 for dirty produce, 99-33 for detained without physical examination).
  • Facility registration current (FSMA food facilities).
  • Sample / lab results returned clean if exam was ordered.

USDA — Animal and Plant Health Inspection Service holds

USDA holds target shipments that may harbor pests, diseases, or prohibited species. This includes live plants, seeds, soil, wood packaging material (WPM), meat, poultry, eggs, and dairy.

Authority: APHIS regulations under 7 CFR Part 319 (plants) and 9 CFR Part 94 (animal products). Wood packaging must meet ISPM 15 marking requirements.

Release criteria:

  • Phytosanitary certificate from the exporting country's NPPO.
  • ISPM 15 mark on all wood pallets/crates (heat treatment or fumigation).
  • No prohibited pests, soil, or animal product detected on inspection.

PGA — Partner Government Agency holds (general)

FDA and USDA are the largest PGAs, but others include CPSC (consumer products), EPA (pesticides, chemicals), NHTSA (vehicles/parts), and TTB (alcohol/tobacco). A generic PGA hold means one of these agencies flagged the entry through CBP's PGA messaging interface.

Release criteria vary by agency but generally require the certificate, test report, or declaration the PGA requested. If you do not know which PGA placed the hold, ask your broker to pull the Cargo Manifest / Entry Summary with the hold code annotated.

How to tell which hold is yours

CBP does not broadcast hold codes to the public in a single public feed. The fastest path is:

  1. Ask your broker for the hold code and the requesting agency.
  2. Log into CBP's IER / ACE portal if you are the importer of record.
  3. Check the FDA FDA Industry Systems (OASIS) for FDA holds.
  4. For USDA, contact the port's APHIS office directly.

What to do today

  1. Pull the exact hold code. EXAM, MAP, and PGA all behave differently. Do not assume a single generic "customs hold" covers all three.
  2. Do not re-book the same drayage. If the hold requires a sample or CES exam, a second trucking appointment may be refused at the gate.
  3. Run the shipment through the ClearanceIQ Compliance Checklist. It maps each hold type to the documents, forms, and CFR citations that release it.
  4. If a PGA is involved, do not guess at the certificate. Contact the specific agency (FDA, USDA, CPSC) for their exact import alert or prior-notice requirement before sending documents.
