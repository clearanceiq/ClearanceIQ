How to Avoid CBP Holds on Your First Shipment

August 19, 2026 · Estimated reading: 6–8 minutes

First-time importers land CBP holds most often on ISF, entry summary, PGA, and bond gaps. Learn the exact filings, CFR citations, and documentation checkpoints that keep a shipment moving.

Why first shipments get held more often

CBP applies the same rules to every entry, but new importers lack the procedural rhythm that experienced buyers build over time. The most expensive surprise is not the duty — it is the hold that generates storage, demurrage, and missed retail windows while paperwork catches up.

The four holds that show up most on first entries

1. ISF / 10+2 hold

CBP requires the Importer Security Filing (ISF) at least 24 hours before cargo is laden aboard a vessel destined for the United States. Failure to file or a materially inaccurate filing triggers a penalty under 19 CFR Part 701 and can lead to an extended hold on the merchandise.

Common ISF errors on first shipments:

  • Buyer (or owner) field left blank or mismatched to the ultimate consignee.
  • HTS code at the 6-digit level omitted or inconsistent with the entry summary.
  • Country of origin missing for each container.

2. Entry summary / manifest discrepancy (MAP)

When the data in the entry summary does not match the manifest data CBP already received, the shipment can land in a MAP (Manifest Analysis Program) hold. This is a data problem, not a physical exam. The fix is usually a corrected or supplemental entry.

Authority: Automated Manifest System (AMS) and 19 CFR Part 141.

3. PGA hold — FDA, USDA, CPSC, EPA

Partner Government Agencies can place holds independently of CBP's targeting. The most common first-shipment PGA holds:

  • FDA — food, dietary supplements, cosmetics, medical devices. Requires Prior Notice for food and a valid facility registration. Authority: FD&C Act, 21 U.S.C. § 381; FSMA, 21 U.S.C. § 350d.
  • USDA / APHIS — live plants, seeds, soil, wood packaging, meat, dairy. Requires phytosanitary certificates and ISPM 15 marking for wood pallets. Authority: 7 CFR Part 319 (plants) and 9 CFR Part 94 (animal products).
  • CPSC — consumer products. Certificate of Conformity or Children's Product Certificate required.

4. Missing or insufficient bond

CBP requires a customs bond before cargo is released. A single transaction bond covers one shipment; a continuous bond covers all entries for one year. The minimum coverage is set under 19 CFR § 113. If the bond is missing or the amount does not cover duties, taxes, and penalties, CBP will not release the cargo.

What you should do today

  1. Confirm ISF is filed and error-free. File at least 24 hours before lading. Verify the 10+2 data elements against your supplier's packing list.
  2. Reconcile the entry summary against the manifest. HTS code, value, and country of origin must match across both documents before the vessel arrives.
  3. Identify PGA requirements by commodity. If you ship food, file FDA Prior Notice now. If you ship wood pallets, confirm ISPM 15 marking. Do not wait for a hold letter to find out.
  4. Buy or verify bond coverage before you book freight. Ask your broker for the bond amount and the surety company. A $50,000 continuous bond is standard for new importers.
  5. Run the shipment through the ClearanceIQ Compliance Checklist. It maps each hold type to the exact document, form, and CFR citation that releases it.
