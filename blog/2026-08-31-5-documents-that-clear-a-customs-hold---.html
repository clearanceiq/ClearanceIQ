5 Documents That Clear A Customs Hold — And The One Most People Forget

August 31, 2026 · Estimated reading: 6–8 minutes

CBP holds release faster when these five documents are complete and consistent. Learn the exact filings, CFR citations, and the one document most importers miss.

Why this matters now

CBP holds are not rare anomalies. In FY 2025, CBP processed more than 25 million entries and issued holds across multiple program lines — from simple manifest discrepancies to PGA-targeted exams. For importers, the cost is immediate: storage accrues at the port, demurrage charges multiply, and retail windows close while paperwork catches up. The fastest release path is a complete, internally consistent document set before the vessel arrives.

Regulatory authority for these requirements spans multiple titles. The core commercial documents are governed by 19 CFR Part 141 (entry requirements) and 19 CFR § 113 (bond requirements). Partner Government Agency holds draw on separate statutes: the FD&C Act for FDA, 7 CFR for USDA/APHIS plant and animal products, and the CPSC requirements for consumer product safety.

The five documents CBP asks for first

1. Commercial Invoice

The commercial invoice is the backbone of the entry. It must show the seller and buyer, a detailed description of the merchandise, the value in US dollars, the currency of sale, incoterms, and the country of origin. Incomplete or inconsistent invoices are the single largest source of MAP (Manifest Analysis Program) holds. Authority: 19 CFR § 141.86.

2. Packing List

The packing list itemizes quantities, weights, and package marks. It does not replace the commercial invoice, but it must be consistent with it. CBP officers compare the packing list against the physical cargo during exam. Discrepancies trigger physical holds and possible penalties under 19 CFR Part 141.

3. Bill of Lading or Air Waybill

The carrier document establishes the contract of carriage and the consignee. It must match the entry summary data element-for-element. Ocean shipments also require the Importer Security Filing (ISF) to be filed at least 24 hours before lading. Authority: 19 CFR Part 701. A missing or inaccurate ISF triggers a $5,000 penalty per shipment and can extend the hold beyond the initial exam line.

4. Entry Summary (CBP Form 7501)

The entry summary is the official accounting of the shipment. It reports HTS codes, values, duties, and any additional information CBP needs for classification and valuation. Errors here — wrong HTS, wrong country of origin, undervaluation — are the most common cause of extended holds and demand letters. Authority: 19 CFR Part 141 and 19 CFR § 142.

5. Customs Bond

A customs bond guarantees payment of duties, taxes, and penalties. Without it, CBP will not release the cargo. Single-transaction bonds cover one shipment; continuous bonds cover all entries for one year. The minimum amount is set under 19 CFR § 113.12. A bond that does not cover the full duty exposure — including potential AD/CVD liabilities — will result in a hold until coverage is increased or verified.

The one most people forget: PGA-specific permits

Even when the five core documents are perfect, a Partner Government Agency (PGA) hold can still freeze the shipment. FDA requires Prior Notice for food and dietary supplements under 21 U.S.C. § 381 and FSMA, 21 U.S.C. § 350d. USDA/APHIS requires phytosanitary certificates and ISPM 15 marking for wood packaging under 7 CFR Part 319 and 9 CFR Part 94. CPSC requires certificates of conformity for consumer products. These permits live outside CBP's core system, so they are easy to overlook until a hold letter arrives.

What you should do today

  1. Audit your commercial invoice against the packing list. Value, weight, and country of origin must match exactly across both documents.
  2. Verify the entry summary data before you file. HTS code, value, and origin must match the manifest and the ISF (if applicable).
  3. Confirm bond coverage. Ask your broker for the bond amount and surety. Make sure it covers AD/CVD exposure, not just the base duty.
  4. File PGA requirements in parallel with CBP entry. If you ship food, file FDA Prior Notice before the shipment arrives. If you ship wood pallets, confirm ISPM 15 marking. Do not wait for a hold letter.
  5. Run the shipment through the ClearanceIQ Compliance Checklist. It maps each required document to the relevant CFR citation and flags PGA requirements by commodity.
